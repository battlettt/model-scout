      3. What to use when

      Rules you can apply without the ranker. Start at the top and stop at the first rule that matches.

      1. Data cannot leave your machines? Use an open-weight model. On a laptop, use a small model (about 8B parameters) for basic chat and extraction. With one strong GPU, a mixture-of-experts model with a few billion active parameters gives much better quality for similar speed. Bigger open models need servers, so price the hardware before you commit.
      2. You need search or similarity, not generated text? Use an embedding model. They cost cents per million tokens. Pick one and stay on it: switching embedding models means re-embedding every document.
      3. High volume, simple answers (classification, tagging, routing, extraction)? Use the cheapest small tier that passes a 50 example test set. Price differences of 10x or more between tiers are common, and simple tasks rarely need a flagship. Ask for short, structured outputs to keep output tokens low.
      4. Answering from your own documents (RAG)? Retrieval quality matters more than model size. Use a mid-tier model and spend effort on retrieval. Only move to long-context models (hundreds of thousands of tokens) when chunking keeps losing the answer, and remember that every token you send is billed.
      5. Whole documents or codebases in one prompt? Filter by context window first: the model must fit your longest input with room for the answer. Then compare input prices, because long prompts are dominated by input cost.
      6. Hard reasoning, tricky code, or work where a mistake is expensive? Use a frontier tier. Check the cost at your real volume: frontier output tokens can cost 50 to 100 times a small tier. A common pattern is a cheap model for the first pass and a frontier model only for the hard cases.
      7. Agents that call tools over many steps? Reliability compounds: a small error rate per step becomes a large one over twenty steps. Favor stronger tiers, and set a cost cap per task.
      8. Real-time chat or autocomplete? Favor fast tiers (names like Flash, Haiku, mini, Lite, Luna). Speed classes here are editorial estimates, so measure time to first token in your own region before you ship.
      9. Still undecided? Pick the cheapest model that passes your test set, then re-run the switch check below every quarter.

      4. Switch triggers: when to reconsider your pick

      • A new model beats yours on your weights. A small score lead is noise. Run a trial when the lead is at least 5 points and the challenger passes your own test set.
      • Pricing changes. Re-run your cost estimate when any provider changes prices. A 2x price cut on a model one tier up can make an upgrade free.
      • Your spec goes stale. Model Scout marks any spec older than 90 days, or with no date, as stale. Re-check stale specs at the source before you rely on them.
      • Your needs change. Longer inputs, a new input type, a privacy requirement, or 10x more volume all change the ranking. Update the profile above and look again.
      • Deprecation notices. Providers retire old versions. Plan the move when the notice arrives, not on the shutdown date.

      5. About the data, and what this app does not do

      • The bundled catalog is dated (loading). Prices and context windows for most models come from OpenRouter's public model list on that date. Buying direct from a provider can cost a different amount.
      • When your browser can reach OpenRouter, Model Scout fetches the live model list and replaces bundled prices with live ones, labelled with the fetch time. If the fetch fails (offline, blocked, or opened from a file with strict browser rules), it uses the bundled catalog and says so at the top.
      • A spec counts as stale when its verification date is missing or older than 90 days. Stale and unverified figures are labelled on every card.
      • Quality tiers and speed classes are editorial estimates from each provider's own positioning of the model (flagship, mid-tier, small). They are not benchmark scores. Model Scout does not publish benchmark numbers.
      • For open-weight models the listed price is what a hosting provider charges. Running the model yourself has no per-token fee, but you pay for hardware and power, which this app does not estimate.
      • Model Scout runs entirely in your browser. It does not call any model, has no accounts, no server, and no tracking. Your task description never leaves the page.
